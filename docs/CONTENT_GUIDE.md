# Content Guide

This document defines how portfolio content should be structured and edited so that case studies remain consistent without becoming formulaic.

## Portfolio goal
Show how research evidence informs design decisions. The portfolio should communicate both methodological rigor and practical design judgment.

## Case-study priorities
A reader should be able to understand, in roughly this order:
1. What the project was.
2. Why the problem mattered.
3. What role and responsibilities were held.
4. How the problem was investigated.
5. What was learned.
6. How those findings changed the design direction.
7. How the work was evaluated.
8. What resulted and what was learned from the project.

## Default case-study structure
This is a starting structure, not a requirement that every project use identical headings.

1. Project overview
2. Context / problem
3. Role, team, and timeline
4. Research approach
5. Key findings
6. Design implications / response
7. Evaluation or iteration
8. Outcome
9. Reflection

## Writing principles
- Lead with the strongest project-specific idea rather than generic process language.
- Explain why a method was used when the reasoning matters.
- Prefer findings and implications over chronological research diaries.
- Connect evidence to design decisions explicitly.
- Avoid claiming certainty that the research did not support.
- Keep technical or methodological detail when it establishes rigor, but avoid turning the page into a full academic report.
- Use short descriptive headings that help scanning.
- Avoid generic UX phrases when a more specific description is available.

## Research evidence
Whenever possible, distinguish among:
- Observation or participant evidence.
- Interpretation or synthesis.
- Design implication.
- Implemented design decision.

Do not invent or embellish metrics, findings, participant quotes, study sizes, or outcomes.

## Figures
Use figures when they help a reader understand:
- A research artifact.
- A workflow.
- A finding.
- A design transition.
- A prototype interaction.
- An evaluation result.

Figures should be placed close to the text they support.

## Project metadata
Likely fields:
- Role.
- Team.
- Timeline.
- Organization/client.
- Methods.
- Tools, when relevant.

Only show metadata that helps a reader understand the work.

## Homepage project summaries
Each project preview should communicate:
- Project/problem area.
- User or research context.
- Primary contribution.
- A reason to open the case study.

Avoid reducing every project to a list of methods.
